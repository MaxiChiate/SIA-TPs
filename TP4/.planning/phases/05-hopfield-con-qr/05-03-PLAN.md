<objective>
The end-to-end tracer of this phase: drive the existing C Hopfield core with the Hebb rule over the five 37x37 QR, under the frozen protocol, from PNG-derived pm-one input to a recovery-vs-noise curve with energy and iterations, independently checked in numpy and rendered to one figure set (HOP-05, and the HOP-04 overlap matrix).

Purpose: prove the full stack works at N=1369 on the real inputs - converter output -> guarded driver -> C binary -> CSVs -> numpy check_recall -> aggregation -> figure - and report honestly whatever Hebb does at zero noise, recording the verdict by applying the frozen D-02 rule to the actual output (the research predicts Hebb does not stabilize the correlated QR; the driver reports the measured result either way). This is the thinnest complete path; pinv and the Hamming baseline (HOP-06) and the remaining limitations (HOP-07) expand from here.

Output: analysis/hopfield_qr.py (guarded Hebb driver writing recall.csv, overlaps.json, qr_facts.json, energy_trace.csv, protocol.json, verdict.json, run.json), analysis/hopfield_qr_figures.py (QR glyphs, 5x5 overlap matrix, Hebb recovery curve, per-step energy panel), analysis/tests/test_hopfield_qr.py, and the make hopfield-qr and make hopfield-qr-figures targets.

Flagged assumptions (surfaced, not resolved):
- HOP-04 (overlap matrix location): the 5x5 overlap matrix and the correlation facts are computed in the driver as deterministic input facts over the pm-one patterns (like Phase 4's orthogonality.json), independent of any run.
- HOP-05 (updates reported): async is the reference; sync is included as a labelled comparison with two-cycle detection (both are cheap and the core already supports them), and energy increases are asserted 0 for async.
</objective>
