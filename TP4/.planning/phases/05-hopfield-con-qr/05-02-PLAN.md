<objective>
Freeze the experiment protocol before the network ever touches the QR (D-01, one-way). Unlike Phase 4 - where the guard protected a selection of letters - here the five QR are fixed and given, so what is pre-registered and committed with a sha256 guard is the experiment protocol itself: the literal hypothesis, the success criterion, the noise grid, the number of trials and seeds, the reported metrics, and the explicit numeric verdict thresholds for "Hebb failed" and "pinv works" (D-02). The guard also freezes the five PNG hashes so neither the protocol nor the images can be retouched after results exist.

Purpose: the research already measured the outcome (PITFALLS.md Pitfall 1: Hebb 0% recovery, pinv the remedy). Pre-registering the protocol and the thresholds, with the same rigor as the committed rules of Phases 2/3/4, keeps the finding from looking fitted after the fact. The protocol is reproduced honestly, not "discovered".

Output: a blocking-human decision checkpoint approving the thresholds, then deliverables/05-hopfield-qr.md section 3 (protocol between the rule markers) and analysis/hopfield_qr_select.py (frozen constants plus a rule_identity guard mirroring analysis/hopfield_select.py), committed together before any run.

Flagged assumptions (surfaced, not resolved):
- HOP-05 (success definition): success is the exact 1369-pixel match (D-03), not an overlap-threshold; the continuous mean final overlap is reported alongside so closeness is still visible even when Hebb never matches exactly.
- HOP-06 (verdict wording): "Hebb fails" is pinned to exact recovery at noise 0 below 100%; "pinv works" to the five QR being exact fixed points (exact recovery at noise 0 == 100%). These are the minimal falsifiable claims; the continuous curves are reported regardless of the verdict.
</objective>
