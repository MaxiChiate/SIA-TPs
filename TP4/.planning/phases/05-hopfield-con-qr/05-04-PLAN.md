<objective>
Expand the proven Hebb tracer into the full three-way comparison the phase is about (HOP-06): add the pseudo-inverse rule run on the five QR over the same frozen noise grid, and a no-dynamics Hamming nearest-neighbour baseline, so the recovery curves of Hebb, pinv and lookup sit on one grid, with energy and iterations for the two dynamical rules.

Purpose: test and report the remedy. The research predicts the pinv rule makes the five correlated QR exact fixed points (the "pinv works" verdict), recovering far into the noise where Hebb gives nothing; the driver records whatever pinv actually does via the frozen D-02 rule. The Hamming 1-NN curve shows a trivial lookup already solves the same closed-set problem - the honest counterweight that feeds the limitations section (D-11c). The three exact-recovery curves are also rendered as a per-level table (D-08).

Output: pinv and Hamming series added to analysis/hopfield_qr.py (three rules in recall.csv), the pinv verdict recorded in verdict.json, the per-level exact-recovery table recovery_table.csv, the three-rule figures in analysis/hopfield_qr_figures.py, and matching tests.

Flagged assumptions (surfaced, not resolved):
- HOP-06 (Hamming tie-break): on equal Hamming distance the baseline returns the lowest stored index (D-08); documented, and ties are vanishingly unlikely given the minimum pairwise distance is well above 1.
- HOP-06 (Gram conditioning): the five QR are linearly independent (p=5 < N=1369), so the Gram inverts; if a future input made it singular the C core returns 0 and the driver surfaces the error (from Plan 01).
</objective>
